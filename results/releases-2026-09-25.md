# Releases with a backend per question: djev, GLiNER or tiny (2026-09-25)

**Setup.** One release per stand-in customer (shop, helpdesk, moderation; built from public data by
`pipeline/samples.py`). Every question had these candidates, each scored on the customer's own held-out rows:
- djev, with calibration, with the customer's rules and examples, or with both;
- GLiNER2.5-Decide with and without calibration;
- Ettin-17M fine-tuned on the training rows ("tiny"), only for questions with at least 20 labelled rows per option.

The bar is plain djev, with no rules, examples or calibration. A candidate replaces the bar for a question only if
its holdout accuracy is at least the bar's and its log-loss is lower. Among the candidates that pass, the lowest
log-loss wins.
Command: `.venv/bin/python pipeline/djp.py release <customer>` (all three arms), then
`.venv/bin/python pipeline/compare.py` for this table. Release records: `data/customers/<c>/releases/`.

**Read this first.**
- Two of six questions gain beyond noise. **shop · category on tiny: 70.8 % → 90.3 %** (19 rows fixed, 5 broken,
  p = 0.007). **helpdesk · type on djev with rules + calibration: 64.1 % → 75.6 %** (p = 0.02).
- moderation · toxic gains 10.5 pts on GLiNER + calibration, but p = 0.12. Treat it as a probable gain, not a
  proven one. Tiny (80.3 %) and djev + calibration (79.0 %) are within 3 rows of it.
- The other three questions move 0–3 pts. That is within noise. A 76–78 row holdout moves 1.3 pts per row.
- The bar itself is noisy. A second, uncached djev read of the same holdouts moved plain-djev accuracy by up to
  5.5 pts ("Bar re-read" column). On that re-read, helpdesk · priority's pick (46.2 %) would fail the accuracy
  test against 48.7 %. The gate reads a frozen djev read per row (`data/customers/<c>/cache/djev.jsonl`). That
  makes its verdict reproducible. It does not make the verdict certain.
- Tiny ran for 5 of 6 questions and won only shop. helpdesk · type has 19 rows for its rarest option, below the
  threshold of 20. On moderation, tiny ties GLiNER. On helpdesk queue and priority, tiny loses to every zero-shot
  candidate. Those labels are noisy synthetic data.
- The gate kept the plain bar for no question. It refused one candidate on log-loss alone: helpdesk · priority
  djev + calibration has 50.0 % accuracy but a higher log-loss than the bar.

**Served = evaluated.** The chosen tiny weights are saved in the release. Served on CPU through
`pipeline/serve.py`, they reproduce the gated holdout accuracy (shop 90.28 %, 72/72 rows answered, p50 5.2 ms per
call). `compare.py` rebuilds every row's prediction from the cache and saved weights. It asserts that each result
equals the released accuracy. It ran with the djev URL pointed at a closed port, so it made no model calls.

**Cost and time.** djev was up 23:00–23:05 IST: a 2 min 4 s cold start, then 3 releases in 2 min 26 s, then 1
re-read. Release wall time with djev reads uncached: shop 44.5 s, helpdesk 44.9 s, moderation 55.5 s. Tiny training
took 2.4 s (shop), 5.0–6.5 s (helpdesk) and 11.6–13.2 s (moderation) per question on Apple MPS. A re-release
after new labels reuses the cached reads. On a copy of shop, `djp.py label` with 30 new rows → retrain → gate →
CURRENT took 5.2 s.
